Adult friendship has no timetable.

School had one. University had one. Then you get a job, and the one structure that ever reliably made friends — same people, same room, every week, no effort required — quietly disappears from your life and nothing replaces it.

So we built one. Same night, same time, same shape, every week. You don't choose your table, you don't have to approach anyone, and you never have to think of something to say — there's a prompt card in front of you and a host running the room. Turning up is the whole of your job.

What actually happens

Same shape every week — only the middle changes, and that's the themed bit. You can walk in cold at 6:55 having never been before and take part in every minute of it.

    6:30

    Name tag at the door: your name, and one thing to ask you about. Then someone walks you to a table. You don't have to find anyone, and nobody is left standing by the wall holding a drink — that's a rule, not a hope.

  2. 7:00

    The room warms up

    7:00

    Four minutes on your feet that gets forty people laughing before anyone has had to make conversation. No talent, no volunteering, no going up front.

  3. 7:10

    Speed friendship

    the bit that makes it work 7:10

    Tables of six, three rounds. Between rounds three of your six get up and move, so you're meeting new people without ever restarting from zero. The prompt cards are already on the table — you never have to invent a question.

  4. 7:55

    7:55

    Buffet opens, included in the ticket. Pasta or pizza, chips, something sweet. Ten minutes where nothing is being run at you.

  5. 8:05

    The main event

    changes weekly 8:05

    Games, a debate, a paint-off, a talent show with no talent in it — whatever that Tuesday is. Always in fresh teams of six, always drawn from people you haven't met yet.

  6. 9:15

    Three names

    9:15

    Everyone writes down three people they'd want to see again. We match the cards up and introduce you properly the next morning. Your number stays yours.

  7. 9:30

    9:30

the honest bit

But what if I just
stand there?

It's the first thing everyone asks, and it's a fair question about most events. So the night is built to make it structurally impossible. You're walked to a table before you've got your coat off. From 7:00 to 9:15 there is always something being run. There is no stretch of the evening where you're expected to circulate and introduce yourself.

Booking a single seat is the normal booking here. You're seated the same way, prompted the same way, and nobody is counting who arrived with whom. If you do bring someone, we'll put you on different tables until the mingle — you came to meet people, not to talk to the one you already know.

Questions people ask

Do people come on their own? +

That's the assumption the whole night is built on. You're walked to a table the moment you arrive and the first hour is entirely structured, so there's no point where you're stood on your own deciding who to approach.

Is this a dating thing? +

No. Fika is for making friends, and we'd rather say that plainly than be coy about it. The room is mixed and tickets are kept roughly balanced, but nothing about the night is built or marketed around romance — no matching, no pairing off, no wink.

Do I need to be good at anything? +

No, and several nights are built so that being bad at it is the joke. Paint night doesn't want painters, salsa doesn't want dancers, and talent night specifically doesn't want talent. Nothing needs preparing beforehand.

What's included in the ticket? +

Entry, the full three hours, and the buffet — pasta or pizza, chips, and something sweet. Everything the night runs on is provided. Drinks from the bar are the only thing you'd pay extra for.

Who is it for? +

People in their twenties and thirties across Nairobi — some who've moved here recently and are starting the friends part from scratch, some who've been here years and hit the stretch where the group chat goes quiet. Tickets are kept roughly balanced so the room never tips one way.

Can I bring a friend? +

Please do. We'll put you on different tables for the first hour and reunite you at the mingle — otherwise you'd spend the evening talking to each other, which you can do any day of the week.

Is there alcohol? +

Fika itself is dry — the night doesn't run on drinking, and it doesn't need to. The venue bar is open if you want something, plenty of people have nothing at all, and nobody notices either way.

How do I book, and where is it? +

Message us on WhatsApp and we'll send you the venue, a pin, and the payment details. Payment holds your seat — we cap the room and cook to a headcount, so we can't hold a place without it.
